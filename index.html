How to read the numbers

Applied and drew. Applied is everyone in your residency group who listed the hunt first or second, with the split underneath. Drew is the tags that group received, split into tags drawn on a first choice and tags drawn on a second choice. The split covers both the bonus pass and the random pass.

Odds. Tags drawn divided by everyone in your residency group who listed the hunt as a first or second choice. Arizona considers both choices on every application, so this is the fair pool. Listing a hunt first does better than this number, and that is what the odds at my points assume. The detail view also shows the first-choice-only ceiling.

Odds at my points. A model of the 2026 draw for one applicant who lists the hunt first with your points. It is not a count of the people who happened to apply at that level. Above the lowest point level that drew a bonus-pass tag, the tag was certain. Below it, the chance comes from the random pass, where each bonus point adds a random number: 1 − (1 − p)points + 1. The per-number chance p is set for each hunt so the model hands first-choice applicants the tags they actually drew. Bull hunts are modeled separately for residents and nonresidents because of the cap. A hunt with tags left over after the pass shows as certain. The result never falls as points rise.

Actual, under the percentage. What happened in 2026 to the applicants at exactly your level, first and second choices together. Small groups bounce around, and second-choice applicants draw less often, so it will not match the model. Both describe 2026 only. Cutoffs move every year with tag numbers and applicants.

All drew at. The lowest point level where every applicant at that level and above drew. A dash means even the top point holders were not all drawn. It can sit above the bonus-pass cutoff, because an applicant who listed the hunt second and drew a first choice counts as not drawing here.

How the draw runs. About 20% of each hunt's tags go first to the highest point holders (the bonus pass). The rest are drawn at random, with one extra random number per bonus point. Nonresidents are capped at about 10% of the tags in each bull hunt, and no more than half of those can come from the bonus pass. Antlerless and any-elk hunts are not held to that; several went above 10% in 2026.

2025 success. Share of hunters who took an elk in the matching 2025 hunt, from the department's hunter survey. Hunts are matched by unit, weapon, elk type and season dates, since hunt numbers change between years. Six 2026 hunts have no 2025 match.

Land access. Game & Fish does not publish land ownership with its draw or harvest reports, so the success rates here do not separate public from private ground. The access tags come from the hunt notes in the 2026 regulations. Unit 10 and Units 15A, 15B and 18A are marked Limited access, because the department warns that access there is restricted and depends on private ranches. By default the table hides those, along with limited-opportunity hunts and small named hunt areas. The Land access filter can hide only the limited-access hunts, or show everything. A hunt with no tag has no access warning in the regulations. That is not a guarantee it is all public, so check a land-ownership map before you apply.

Not included. Hopi and Camp Navajo hunts, over-the-counter nonpermit hunts, and tags issued in the third-to-fifth-choice pass.
